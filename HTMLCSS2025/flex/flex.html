Put your foot down, once an’ for all, an’ be the mistress of your own house an’ the boss of your own family. Mrs Pearson: [smiling apologetically] That’s easier said than done. Besides I’m so fond of them even if they are so thoughtless and selfish. They don’t mean to be... Mrs Fitzgerald: [cutting in] Maybe not. But it’ud be better for them if they learnt to treat you properly... Mrs Pearson: Yes, I suppose it would, in a way. Mrs Fitzgerald: No doubt about it at all. Who’s the better for being spoilt— grown man, lad or girl? Nobody. You think it does ’em good when you run after them all the time, take their orders as if you were the servant in the house, stay at home every night while they go out enjoying themselves? Never in all your life. It’s the ruin of them as well as you. Husbands, sons, daughters should be taking notice of wives an’ mothers, not giving ’em orders an’ treating ’em like dirt. An’ don’t tell me you don’t know what I mean, for I know more than you’ve told me. Mrs Pearson: [dubiously] I—keep dropping a hint... Mrs Fitzgerald:Hint?
